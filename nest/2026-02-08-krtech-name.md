# Is "krte-ch" a Good Name?

**Question:** Is krte-ch actually a good name or does it matter anyway?

**Date:** 2026-02-08

---

## The Name Itself

**Pros:**
- Clever wordplay: "krtek" (beloved Czech mole) + "tech" = krte-ch
- .ch domain is Switzerland (premium, trusted TLD)
- Short, 7 characters with the hyphen
- Has personality — not generic startup slop

**Cons:**
- Pronunciation unclear for non-Czechs ("kurt-ech"? "kir-tech"?)
- The hyphen is awkward — is it krte.ch the domain, or "krte-ch" the brand?
- Nobody outside Czech Republic will get the krtek reference
- Verbally sharing it: "It's k-r-t-e dash c-h" — not smooth

**Honest verdict:** It's *fine*. Not great, not terrible. The clever Czech reference is cute but invisible to 99% of potential users.

---

## Does It Actually Matter Right Now?

**No.** Here's why:

1. **You need Apple Developer enrollment, not brand equity.** Apple cares that you're a registered business, not that your name sounds good.

2. **Your apps have their own names.** FX Europe and Stampede PDF will live or die by *their* names and quality. The parent company name is mostly for invoices and legal.

3. **You can always rebrand later.** Plenty of companies start as "Generic Holdings Ltd" and build real brands for products.

4. **Analysis paralysis is the real enemy.** Every week spent debating names is a week not shipping.

---

## Bottom Line

**The name is fine. Ship it.**

If you already own krte.ch or can get it, use it. If Patrik likes it and you like it — that's enough for an entity that exists mainly to sign legal documents and collect payments.

The time to obsess over company branding is when you're raising money or the company IS the product. Right now it's an infrastructure decision, not a marketing one.
